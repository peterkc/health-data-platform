## Evidence

- CMS-0062-P is a proposed rule. Comments closed June 15, 2026. It has no final
  effective date and none of its proposals should be stated as current law.
- HHS proposes FHIR and associated guides as HIPAA standards for specified
  referral certification and authorization and eligibility transactions related
  to prior authorization.
- That HIPAA proposal would bind providers, plans, and clearinghouses that engage
  in the specified electronic transactions. Nonparticipants would not have to
  adopt it. Proposed compliance is 24 months after a final rule takes effect, or
  36 months for small plans.
- CDex is proposed for attachments accompanying HIPAA prior-authorization
  transactions, including C-CDA, PDF, and text.
- Medical-benefit drugs would use the FHIR Prior Authorization API. Pharmacy-
  benefit drugs would use NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, and Real-Time
  Prescription Benefit standards. The proposed rule calls these sets mutually
  exclusive for their respective benefit channels.
- CMS proposes making currently recommended CRD, DTR, and PAS guides mandatory
  for applicable APIs beginning October 1, 2027. The newer versions remain tied
  to ONC adoption and final rulemaking.
- CMS proposes a centralized directory of payer API endpoints, capability
  statements, authentication documentation, and registration information.
  Changes would be reported within one week.
- Proposed drug and API-usage metrics would begin in 2028 for 2027 data.
- Five RFIs cover ADT notifications, cybersecurity resilience, payer API testing
  and oversight, step therapy, and prior authorization for laboratory tests and
  DMEPOS. RFIs are questions for future policy, not requirements.

## Notes

The proposal broadens future interoperability but does not collapse the market
into one protocol. Medical-benefit and pharmacy-benefit drugs follow different
standards, and any compliance dates tied to a future final rule remain
conditional. The endpoint directory and payer API oversight questions expose an
operational opportunity, but neither is a final obligation today.
